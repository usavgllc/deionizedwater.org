What is Deionized Water?

Deionized water (DI water, DIW or de-ionized water), often synonymous with demineralized water, is water that has had almost all of its mineral ions removed. This includes cations like sodium, calcium, iron, and copper, and anions such as chloride and sulfate.

Because the majority of water impurities are dissolved salts, deionization produces high-purity water that is generally similar to distilled water, but produced faster and without the scale buildup common in distillation units.

How Deionization Works

Deionization is a chemical process that uses specially manufactured ion-exchange resins. These resins exchange hydrogen (H+) and hydroxyl (OH-) ions for dissolved minerals, which then combine to form water (H2O).

  • Cation Exchange: Removes positively charged ions.
  • Anion Exchange: Removes negatively charged ions.
  • Mixed Bed Deionization: Uses both resins in a single column for the highest purity levels.

Understanding purification methods is essential when choosing the right deionization approach. Many off-the-shelf DI systems can leave trace organic contaminants or bacteria if not properly maintained. Read our comparison of RO, DI, and distillation methods to understand the differences.

Purity Grades & Standards

Not all DI water is created equal. The industry typically follows ASTM D1193 or ISO 3696 standards to classify water quality.

Applications of DI Water

DI water is utilized across a vast range of industries where mineral-free water is a requirement:

  • Laboratory: Reagent preparation, glassware rinsing, and instrument feed.
  • Industrial: Cooling systems, lead-acid batteries, and electroplating.
  • Electronics: Semiconductor wafer cleaning and circuit board manufacturing.
  • Pharmaceutical: Ingredient water for non-sterile products and cleaning validation.
